Before you apply for a permit
  1. A section 19 permit is only for units concerned with education, religion, social welfare, recreation or other activities of benefit to the community.
  2. Standard permits cover vehicles adapted to carry no more than 16 passengers, excluding the driver.
  3. The permit covers your members and the people your unit exists to help. It can’t be used to carry members of the general public.
  4. MSSC applies under the non-commercial purposes exemption. Read the section 19 guidance on GOV.UK.
  5. You’re responsible for keeping vehicles maintained and using drivers with the right qualifications.
  6. Have ready: your charity or company number (if any), maintenance garage details and any existing permit or licence numbers.
  7. Questions? Email motortransport@ms-sc.org or call SCC Stores on 023 8251 5345.
Giving false information or modifying this form to obtain a permit is an offence and could lead to prosecution or your vehicle being impounded.

A permit must not be used with a view to profit nor incidentally to an activity which is itself carried on for profit.

For example, an organisation won’t be operating for profit if it runs a service from sheltered accommodation to the local supermarket.

But, if the supermarket offered to pay for the service, this service would be operated incidental to an activity which in itself is carried on with a view to profit, i.e. the supermarket. This would not be allowed under a permit.

Declaration

Read the declaration, then sign on behalf of your unit.

I am authorised by the unit applying for this permit to make this declaration on behalf of its members (or members of the local branch or group named in this application).

The unit will operate under this permit within the terms of section 19 of the Transport Act 1985 and any regulations made under it. The vehicle will only be used where it is engaged in road passenger transport exclusively for non-commercial purposes, and the laws relating to the driving and operation of vehicles used under this permit will be observed.

Any vehicle used under the permit, owned or hired in, will:

  • comply with the appropriate construction requirements and conditions of fitness
  • be kept in a fit and serviceable condition, with regular maintenance inspections
  • have mechanical faults rectified promptly and before it is used again
  • be properly insured for the uses authorised by the permit

Drivers will:

  • report mechanical faults as soon as possible
  • hold the appropriate entitlement and qualification to drive a PSV under the permit
  • be informed of their legal responsibilities as the driver of a vehicle used under a permit

The unit will notify motortransport@ms-sc.org within 28 days of any change that might affect the permit, if issued.

As conditions of the permit, the unit will:

  • continue to meet the not-for-profit and social welfare requirements
  • keep records showing it continues to be an exempt unit within the meaning of the Transport Act 1985 and Regulation (EC) No 1071/2009 (Retained), and make them available on request to a traffic commissioner or any enforcement body
  • only use vehicles with over nine passenger seats under the permits
  • ensure adequate maintenance and facilities, as per the DVSA Guide to Maintaining Roadworthiness
  • make sure vehicles receive the correct annual test for their class
  • require drivers to hold the correct entitlement, including under the Vehicle Drivers (Certificates of Professional Competence) Regulations 2007/605, the Community Bus Regulations 2009 and the Transport Act 1985
  • comply with drivers’ hours rules, specifically Regulation (EC) No 561/2006 as amended by Regulation (EC) No 1073/2009 and (EC) 165/2014
Failure to comply with the conditions or undertakings on a permit can result in regulatory action against the permit holder, and failure to comply with conditions is a criminal offence.
